### Design Statement — 650 / 650

```text
Night Crossing is a self-paced browser game for a quiet break through exploration and small acts of care. Aboard a ring-shaped night ship, players grow flowering plants, assemble them into living lamps and deliver them to isolated places. A crossing leaves time to make tea, draw on a misted window or connect stars beneath the observation dome. Simple gestures create small changes in light, sound and movement, and each delivery leaves a light in the world outside. A guided first request gives players a clear beginning; the rooms invite them to linger at their own pace. The voyage connects curiosity and care to a lasting change beyond the ship.
```

### Idea — 650 / 650

```text
For adults seeking a quiet interval in a busy day, Night Crossing makes it easy to begin with one manageable act: someone along the route has asked for a lamp. The request gives exploration a purpose, while the time between destinations leaves room for curiosity. Caring for a plant, preparing a warm drink or waiting for faint stars turns familiar actions into opportunities to notice and respond. The slightly fantastical setting creates distance from daily pressures, and players choose how deeply to engage, how long to stay and when to leave. Through a journey that welcomes both making and lingering, everyday care becomes a source of pleasure.
```

### Form — 645 / 650

```text
Painterly surfaces generated at runtime and three-dimensional rooms give the night ship the intimacy of an illustrated world with the depth of a place to explore. Warm lamplight makes working areas legible against cool blue metal and a dark sky. Paper charts, handwritten sheets and pencil marks carry the rooms' material character into menus and instructions. Composed viewpoints keep objects close, while looking around and leaning in reveal spatial depth. Synthesized ambience, glass sounds, leaf movements and the pace of a closing door give small actions their physical weight. Image, sound and timing sustain the same unhurried atmosphere.
```

### Function — 649 / 650

```text
A first-delivery guide leads players from a request to the sea chart, workbench and handover, with each step tied to the next usable object. Choosing a destination starts the crossing while other rooms remain available. At the bench, a flowering plant becomes a lamp through visible assembly; at the gangway, offering it and letting go are separate actions. Mouse and touch input, contextual actions and composed views keep interaction close to its result. Landscape and portrait views adapt to different screens. Sound and motion settings support different sensory preferences, and automatic saving lets players leave and return to the same voyage.
```

### Differentiation — 646 / 650

```text
Small actions in Night Crossing connect the room, the journey and the world beyond it. A plant becomes a lamp, a visitor takes it, and its light remains at the destination after the ship leaves. Within that larger arc, the world responds to attention: a fast hand closes leaves, a slower one lets them reopen; dimming a cabin reveals the view outside; darkness gradually brings faint stars into sight. Mist gathers where glass is held, then parts along a drawn line to reveal the night outside. These linked relationships turn pace and perception into ways of exploring, giving quiet activities consequences the player can discover and return to.
```

### Sustainability — 646 / 650

```text
Night Crossing treats digital leisure as time the player can shape and set aside. Automatic saving supports short visits and a continuing voyage; a dry plant waits for water and resumes growing when cared for. English and Chinese interfaces, landscape and portrait layouts, and sound and motion controls make the same world available across different preferences and situations. Players choose whether to write postcards or name constellations, keeping personal expression voluntary and open-ended. The experience makes patient attention, everyday care and consideration for others part of ordinary play, with a natural place to pause and return.
```
